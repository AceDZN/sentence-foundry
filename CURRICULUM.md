# Sentence Foundry curriculum

This original construction game uses the comprehension scope of two saved Workbook English modules for Israeli grade 8. The saved source snapshots are in `docs/curriculum`. Game contract sentences below are original authored fixtures, not quotations from Workbook. This slice practices reading to make construction decisions. It does not establish grammar production, speaking mastery, Ministry approval, or measured learning gains.

Sources:

- [Who Does It and What Is Done](https://english.workbook.co.il/grade-8/chapter-7/who-does-it-and-what-is-done): present simple active/passive, agent and receiver, present auxiliary plus participle, and omitted agents.
- [Following a Production Process](https://english.workbook.co.il/grade-8/chapter-7/following-a-production-process): sequence, same-material reference, and causal reasons for stages.

## Truth and validation

`src/content.ts` keeps text separate from each `ProductionSpec`. The spec is an authored semantic truth graph: receiver, effective action witnesses, any named agents, partial order edges, and final product properties. `src/semantics.ts` supplies immutable physical operations and acceptance. It does not parse arbitrary English, judge student grammar, or use runtime AI.

The normalized label operation targets the product receiving a label; in “Labels are attached to the crates,” the labels are the grammatical subject and the crates are the destination. The game does not teach that every grammatical subject is the product. The game separates a fixed operating painter from the detached `service-arm` workpiece. Washing and painting a packed item have no effect on the sealed contents; an ineffective operation cannot witness a required event. A label placed before packing is on the item; one placed after packing is on the package. These distinctions are physical state, visible feedback, and testable meaning.

Tests independently assert active/passive invariance, receiver and named-agent swaps, omitted-agent freedom, pronoun receivers, agreement and participle fixtures, destructive wash/paint counterexamples, sealed contents, new transfer dependencies, correct rework, invalid truth graphs, and exhaustive short operation-sequence solvability. Grid routing, collision/jam recovery, and browser completion are verified separately by the lead's simulation and playtests.

## Reading support and evidence limits

Hebrew help and vocabulary translations support reading without replacing task-bearing English with a coordinate solution. Contract reading, planning, pause and vocabulary inspection carry no time penalty. The transfer help only invites rereading, without disclosing its sequence. A supported attempt and an unaided first choice must remain distinct in local events; production-time or footprint medals describe factory performance, not comprehension. Receiving English instructions and completing a route do not prove that a learner can independently produce active/passive grammar.
